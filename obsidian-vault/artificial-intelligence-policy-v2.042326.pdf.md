# Artificial Intelligence Policy

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes guidelines for the ethical, safe, and lawful use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, systems, and applications within Anglo Singapore International School (Anglo). It ensures compliance with the Thailand Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) B.E. 2562 (2019) and international best practices in educational technology.

## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
- Teachers
- Administrative staff and interns
- Students

## 3. Definitions
- **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**: Any system or software that performs tasks requiring human-like decision-making, prediction, or content generation, including but not limited to:
  - ChatGPT
  - Google Gemini
  - Copilot
  - Adaptive learning software
- **Automated Decision-Making (ADM)**: Any process where outcomes significantly affecting individuals are made by AI with limited human oversight.
- **Generative AI**: AI that produces new text, images, code, or other media based on training data.
- **Sensitive Personal Data**: As defined by PDPA Section 26, includes information about health, biometrics, religion, etc.

## 4. Guiding Principles
Anglo Singapore International School is committed to ensuring that all AI use follows these principles:
1. **Transparency**: Ensure transparency about where and how AI is used. Parents and students will be informed when they are interacting with an AI system or when AI is used to support a decision.
2. **Explainability**: AI tools must provide understandable reasons for their outputs. Teachers must be able to explain to a student why an AI tool provided a specific piece of feedback.
3. **Repeatability/Reproducibility**: AI systems used for student learning or school operations must yield consistent and stable results under similar conditions to ensure reliability.
4. **Safety**: AI tools must operate within safe parameters, ensuring that students are never exposed to harmful content or dangerous instructions.
5. **Security**: All AI applications must be protected against unauthorized access and "prompt injections" that could reveal internal school data or student records.
6. **Robustness**: AI tools must be technically resilient and perform reliably across various classroom scenarios without failing or producing errors.
7. **Fairness**: Users must be committed to identifying and mitigating algorithmic bias to ensure that AI feedback and adaptive learning paths are equitable for all students, regardless of their background.
8. **Data Governance**: AI usage must strictly adhere to the school’s Data Protection Policy and Thailand's PDPA. This includes ensuring high-quality data is used and that data must never be used to train public AI models.
9. **Accountability**: While AI may assist in tasks, the final responsibility for any AI-augmented decision—such as a grade or a behavioral report—rests with the school leadership and teachers.
10. **Human Agency and Oversight**: Users must maintain a "Human-in-the-Loop" approach. AI will never replace human judgment for significant decisions; a qualified staff member must always have the power to override an AI output.
11. **Inclusive Growth, Societal and Environmental Well-being**: AI should be used to benefit the entire school community, fostering an inclusive environment and ensuring its use does not negatively impact our local or global society and environment.
12. AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Co-Pilot) should:
   - Support learning, not replace thinking.
   - Promote originality and prevent plagiarism.
   - Align with Anglo’s HQLT model (Dignified, Meaningful, Independent Learning).
   - Develop students’ critical, ethical, and responsible use of technology.

## 5. Approved Use of AI
AI may be used in the following contexts with DPO oversight:
- **Learning and Teaching**: Adaptive learning, language tutoring, and plagiarism detection tools.
- **Operations**: Data analytics for attendance, scheduling, and resource optimization.
- **Administration**: Predictive analysis for enrollment trends, not individual student profiling.

## 6. Prohibited Use of AI
The following are strictly prohibited without explicit leadership approval:
- Processing biometric data for surveillance, attendance, or discipline without lawful basis and parental consent (Thailand PDPA Sections 24 & 26).
- Using AI to make automated decisions that significantly affect students or staff, including but not limited to:
  - Academic grading
  - Disciplinary action
- Uploading or sharing personal data of students or staff into public AI models.
- Generating, sharing, or promoting inappropriate, biased, or discriminatory content using AI tools.
- Using unverified AI software or apps, or deploying them on school systems.

## 7. Data Protection & Compliance
- Teachers and staff must ensure that no sensitive personal data is entered into AI systems unless it is authorized and encrypted.
- Students have the right not to be subjected to decisions based purely on automated processing where it produces a legal or significant effect.
- Any suspected data exposure or misuse through AI tools must be reported immediately to the DPO.

## 8. Responsible Use by Teachers and Staff
### A. Teacher and Staff Ethical Use
- AI should be used to enhance, not replace, human teaching and decision-making.
- All educational materials generated by AI must be reviewed for accuracy, age appropriateness, and bias before use.
- Staff must cite AI use when generating content or recommendations.
- AI accounts used for school purposes must be registered with official school email accounts only.
- Staff and teachers must avoid uploading student work, personal data, or internal documents into AI tools.
- Teachers are in a "privileged position of trust and respect" and have a special responsibility to ensure AI tools are used to maintain that trust.
- Teachers must:
  - During the first week of each term, explain what AI can and cannot do.
  - Model acceptable prompts.
  - Discuss copyright, bias, and misinformation.
  - Ask students to differentiate between AI assistance and AI dependence.

### B. Design AI-Resilient Assessment Tasks
To maintain integrity, teachers must:
- Require handwritten components.
- Include oral defenses, viva-style questions.
- Ask students to connect to personal experience or Anglo-specific examples.
- Use classroom-based performance tasks.
- Use process-based submissions: draft - revision - final.

### C. Use AI for Teacher Work, Not Student Learning Outcomes
Teachers may use AI for:
- Lesson planning.
- Creating worksheets.
- Drafting rubrics.
- Generating quizzes.
- Explaining concepts for teacher preparation.

BUT teachers must not:
- Present AI-generated content as “teacher-made” without checking accuracy.
- Use AI to assign tasks that are too complex or inappropriate for students.
- Use AI to generate formative assessments.

## 9. Responsible Use by Students
- Students may use AI tools under teacher supervision.
- Students must not submit AI-generated content as original work without proper attribution.
- AI use must not involve uploading personal information or photos to external AI systems.
- Students must not rely on AI to bypass learning.
- Please see The Use of Generative AI in Student Work Policy for more information.

## 10. Monitoring and Accountability
- The school has the responsibility to protect teachers, staff, and students' data and maintain network security. As such, the school will use technical safeguards to ensure that AI use on school networks complies with data protection laws and school policy.
- Monitoring will be carried out solely for data protection, cybersecurity, and policy-compliance purposes in accordance with the Thailand Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) and the school’s IT and Privacy Policies.
- Monitoring tools will be implemented only within school networks and are limited to AI usage metadata only (visited domain names, access time, and network hotspot account identifiers).
- Monitoring tools will not access or store personal content from the devices (school-owned or personally owned) and do not include personal communications.
- Teachers and staff must report any AI-related incident or concern via the Breach Response Process (see Data Protection Policy - Section 13).
- Regular audits will evaluate AI usage compliance and PDPA alignment.

## 11. Non-Compliance and Restorative Actions
Any data breach involving Artificial Intelligence will follow the centralized Data Breach & Violation Protocol established in Section 13 of the Data Protection Policy. However, the school’s response to AI-specific incidents will be addressed through empowerment, coaching, and training before any formal measures are considered, unless intentional. Depending on the severity, restorative actions may include:
- Written warning with mandatory retraining.
- Suspension of IT or account privileges with mandatory retraining.
- Repeated or serious misuse which may or may not involve data breaches or ethical violations will be managed under the school’s Staff Code of Ethics or the Use of Generative AI for Students framework.

## 12. Review and Training
- This policy will be reviewed annually by the DPO and updated as AI regulations evolve.
- Staff and students will receive AI awareness and data ethics training yearly.
- The school will monitor local and global AI regulations for future alignment.
