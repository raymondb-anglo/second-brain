**Period 1: Fine Arts – Figure Sculpture**  
Figure sculpture is a three-dimensional artistic practice that explores the human form through various materials such as clay, metal, wood, and stone. Understanding human proportions is essential, with classical guidelines like the Vitruvian Man providing a foundation for balance and realism. Artists employ techniques such as additive (building up material), subtractive (removing material), and casting (creating a mold) to shape figures that can be either expressive or anatomically accurate. Beyond simply replicating physical likeness, figure sculpture conveys movement, emotion, and narrative, making it a powerful medium for artistic storytelling and representation.

**Period 2: World History – The Industrial Revolution**  
This lesson explores the rapid transition from agrarian societies to industrialized, urban centers starting in 18th-century Britain. Students will examine key technological innovations such as the steam engine, the spinning jenny, and the factory system. The focus will be on the socio-economic impacts of these advancements, including the rise of the working class, changes in labor laws, urbanization, and the environmental consequences of early mass production. Students will engage in discussions about how these changes shaped modern society and the ongoing implications of industrialization.

**Period 3: Earth Science – Plate Tectonics**  
This topic covers the dynamic movement of the Earth's lithosphere and how it shapes our planet's surface. Students will learn about the different types of plate boundaries: divergent, convergent, and transform. Each type is associated with specific geological phenomena, such as earthquakes, volcanic activity, and mountain building. By studying the fossil record and the concept of Pangaea, students will gain an understanding of the historical evidence supporting continental drift and how it has influenced Earth's geological history.

**Period 4: English Literature – The Hero's Journey**  
This lesson introduces Joseph Campbell’s concept of the "Monomyth" or the Hero's Journey. Students will analyze the archetypal stages of this narrative structure, including the Call to Adventure, the Supreme Ordeal, and the Return. By comparing classical myths with contemporary stories, students will develop a critical framework for understanding character development and plot progression in universal storytelling. This exploration will help them appreciate the common themes that resonate across cultures and time periods.
